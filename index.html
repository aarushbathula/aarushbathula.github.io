Research Statement

The inaccessibility of housing for younger generations motivates my research on how housing markets constrain economic growth. My dissertation develops a micro-founded endogenous growth model incorporating housing frictions, augmenting the Solow-Swan framework to analyze spatial misallocation effects. Under Dr. Arthur Galichère's supervision, I employ numerical methods in MATLAB and Dynare to solve and calibrate theoretical models, combining dynamic optimization with empirical validation.

My research integrates economic theory, computational methods, and causal inference. I apply advanced econometric techniques (instrumental variables, regression discontinuity, limited dependent variable models) to policy-relevant questions in housing, labor markets, and development. My empirical approach follows frontier research in applied microeconomics. I aim to contribute methodologically rigorous research at the intersection of computational economics, applied statistics, and policy evaluation.

  • 2025 Sibling Gender Effects on Academic Self-Perception in Belgium Stata

    Grade: 78% | Ranked 1st/90 students

    Applied ordered probit models to HBSC survey data (2006, 2010, 2014) exploiting quasi-random assignment of sibling sex composition following Angrist & Evans (1998). Conducted extensive robustness checks including heterogeneity analysis by age, gender, and family affluence. Performed power calculations to assess minimum detectable effects.

    Key Finding: Found no statistically significant effect of sibling gender on academic performance (marginal effects < 1 percentage point, all p > 0.10), suggesting educational policy should focus on socioeconomic factors rather than family composition.

  • 2025 Country-of-Origin Effects on UK Immigrant Occupational Outcomes Stata

    Grade: 78% | Top 5% of 422 students

    Estimated ordered probit and multinomial logit models on 2021 UK Census microdata (993,034 observations) to analyze occupational skill-matching by migrant origin. Conducted two-stage analysis: (1) country-level marginal effects from probit models, (2) OLS regressions on log GDP per capita and migration distance. Performed extensive robustness checks including RESET tests, VIF analysis, and Huber-White standard errors.

    Key Findings: 1% increase in origin GDP per capita raises high-skill probability by 3.6pp (p<0.01); 1,000km distance increase raises high-skill probability by 10pp (p<0.01). Former British colonies show 18.3pp higher high-skill placement (p<0.05). Post-Brexit migrants from Western origins show significantly improved high-skill outcomes (US: +0.334 to +0.584).

    Methods: Ordered probit, multinomial logit, two-stage regression, heteroskedasticity-robust inference.

  • 2024 Mental Health Medication and Body Mass Index R

    Grade: 88% | Ranked 1st/558 students

    Analyzed 4,358 observations from the 2019 Health Survey for England to estimate the relationship between prescribed mental health medications (antidepressants, antipsychotics, hypnotics) and BMI. Performed hypothesis testing using t-tests and non-parametric Mann-Whitney U tests. Conducted Shapiro-Wilk normality tests and diagnostic checks for heteroskedasticity.

Economic Growth Theory

  • 2025 Technology Transfer and Convergence in Sub-Saharan Africa Theory

    Grade: 72%

    Theoretical analysis of convergence prospects for Sub-Saharan African economies using endogenous growth frameworks. Modeled technology diffusion through FDI and international trade as mechanisms for knowledge spillovers. Applied Schumpeterian growth models with creative destruction to analyze sectoral catch-up dynamics and technology frontier effects. Examined how absorptive capacity constraints (human capital, infrastructure, institutional quality) determine the effectiveness of cross-country knowledge transfer.

    Key Argument: Conditional convergence requires SSA economies to develop sufficient absorptive capacity to translate technology spillovers into sustained productivity growth. Without domestic innovation effort and institutional reform, imported technology faces an asymptotic productivity limit, preventing permanent catch-up to frontier economies. The model shows that trade integration (African Continental Free Trade Area) combined with FDI attraction and human capital investment creates necessary but not sufficient conditions for convergence, which ultimately depends on eliminating extractive institutions and building innovation capacity.

Research Interests & Future Goals

My research aims to bridge economic theory and computational methods. I follow frontier research in macroeconomics through the Institute for Fiscal Studies and engage with contemporary economic thinking through Greg Mankiw's blog. My work sits at the intersection of housing economics, urban policy, and macroeconomic growth, bringing rigorous computational methods and causal inference to questions of spatial inequality and development.

Beyond my current dissertation, I aim to develop fluency as a hybrid researcher combining computational mathematics with economic analysis. This means mastering advanced numerical methods (optimization algorithms, dynamic programming, stochastic simulation) alongside structural economic modeling and policy evaluation. I seek to contribute methodologically at the frontier of computational economics, where mathematical rigor meets real-world policy questions. My goal is to build models that are both theoretically sound and computationally tractable, translating complex economic problems into solvable mathematical frameworks that inform evidence-based policy design.
