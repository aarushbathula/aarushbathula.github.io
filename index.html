Research Statement

The inaccessibility of housing motivates my research on how resource constraints interact with dynamic systems to shape long-run economic outcomes. My dissertation develops a micro-founded growth model with housing as a binding capital constraint, extending the Solow–Swan framework to study spatial misallocation. The model is solved numerically using fixed-point iteration, steady-state calibration, and simulation, implemented in MATLAB and Dynare.

More broadly, my work sits at the intersection of applied mathematics, statistical inference, and economic modelling. Empirically, I use transparent identification strategies—instrumental variables, regression discontinuity, and limited dependent variable models implemented using maximum likelihood and modern computational workflows. My goal is to develop research that is methodologically rigorous, computationally explicit, and reproducible, translating complex economic questions into formally solvable and statistically testable frameworks.

Ongoing Research

  • 2025 Rethinking Endogenous Growth Models: Housing Frictions and Spatial Misallocation MATLAB · Dynare

    Developing a discrete-time growth model where housing capital \(H_t\) acts as a binding constraint on effective labour.

    Production: $$Y_t = K_t^{\alpha} H_t^{\beta} (A_{\text{eff},t} L_t)^{1-\alpha-\beta}$$

    I solve the equilibrium numerically (fixed-point iteration and calibration) to analyse how housing supply inelasticity amplifies misallocation and slows convergence.

    Supervisor: Dr. Arthur Galichère

    Abstract
    I construct a two-capital growth model in which housing enters production as a congestion-limiting constraint. The equilibrium is characterised implicitly and solved numerically using fixed-point iteration. Simulated impulse responses illustrate how housing supply inelasticity amplifies spatial misallocation and slows convergence.

Research Interests & Future Goals

My research aims to bridge applied mathematics, statistical inference, and economic modelling. I am particularly interested in problems where equilibrium conditions must be solved numerically and where identification relies on careful statistical design rather than parametric assumptions.

I follow frontier research in macroeconomics through the Institute for Fiscal Studies and engage with contemporary economic thinking through Greg Mankiw's blog.

Going forward, I aim to deepen my training in computational mathematics and applied statistics, focusing on numerical optimisation, simulation, and algorithmic approaches to model solution and estimation. My goal is to work on problems where mathematical structure and computation are central to understanding real-world systems.
