Growth, Housing, and Theory

2025 Working paper Macro · Urban MATLAB · Dynare
Rethinking Endogenous Growth: Housing Frictions and Spatial Misallocation

Contribution: A growth framework where housing scarcity and frictions shape spatial allocation, productivity, and innovation—solved numerically and designed for calibration.

Supervisor: Dr. Arthur Galichère · Methods: steady states, numerical solution, impulse responses, calibration-ready structure.
Abstract

This paper develops a growth model that embeds housing constraints and market frictions into spatial allocation. A matching block can be represented as \(M_t = \chi (H^{vac}_t)^\mu B_t^{1-\mu}\) and used to derive an implicit occupancy condition. 2025 Course paper Growth theory Theory
Technology Transfer and Convergence in Sub-Saharan Africa

Contribution: A theory-driven analysis of catch-up dynamics emphasizing absorptive capacity and the limits of imported technology without domestic innovation and institutional reform.

Abstract

I study convergence prospects through endogenous growth mechanisms where knowledge diffusion via trade/FDI interacts with domestic constraints (human capital, institutions, and innovation capacity). The central implication is that integration is necessary but not sufficient: without domestic absorptive capacity, technology adoption faces diminishing returns and cannot deliver permanent catch-up.

Econometrics & Causal Inference

2025 Course paper (revised) Applied micro Stata
Sibling Gender Effects on Academic Self-Perception in Belgium

Contribution: Tests quasi-random sibling sex composition for effects on academic self-perception; emphasizes robustness and power to interpret null results credibly.

Methods: ordered probit, robustness/heterogeneity, minimum detectable effects. · Output: replication-ready Stata workflow.
Abstract

Using HBSC survey data and quasi-random variation in sibling sex composition, I estimate ordered response models for academic self-perception. I report robustness checks and heterogeneity analyses, and complement inference with minimum detectable effects to clarify what magnitudes are ruled out.

2025 Course paper Migration · Labour Stata
Country-of-Origin Effects on UK Immigrant Occupational Outcomes

Contribution: Quantifies how origin-country characteristics predict skill matching in UK occupations using large-scale Census microdata and a transparent two-stage approach.

Methods: ordered probit / multinomial logit, two-stage regression, robust inference. · Data: UK Census microdata (large-N).
Abstract

I estimate occupational skill placement models using Census microdata, then relate country-level marginal effects to origin GDP per capita and migration distance. The design emphasizes interpretability: clear marginal effects, diagnostic checks, and robustness to specification.
