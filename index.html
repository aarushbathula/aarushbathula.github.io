Work In Progress

Housing Frictions, Spatial Misallocation, and Long-Run Growth 2025-present. MATLAB. Dynare.

A quantitative model of housing frictions, urban search, and endogenous growth, in which rural households, urban searchers, and housed urban residents interact through vacancy matching, rent bargaining, and housing investment.

Supervisor: Dr. Arthur Galichère

Abstract
I study a three-location economy with rural households, urban searchers, and housed urban residents. Housing access is mediated by search and matching frictions, landlords and tenants bargain over rents, and developers choose investment subject to construction and vacancy costs. Because frontier productivity growth rises with the housed urban share, the model links housing market frictions to spatial misallocation, under-urbanisation, and slower long-run growth.
